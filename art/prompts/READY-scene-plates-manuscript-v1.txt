Candidate medium for environments. Pairs with the line-and-fill figure sheets.

WHAT THIS IS, AND THE ONE ADAPTATION THAT MATTERS

Pentiment's grammar, and 1775's sources.

The grammar is what makes that game cohere and it is entirely portable: a
hand-drawn ink contour round everything, flat opaque colour inside it, no
rendering, no cast shadows, no gradients, and a deliberately flattened space in
which things are stacked up the page rather than receding into it. World and
people drawn in one language, so a figure standing in a scene belongs to it.

The sources are NOT portable, and copying them would be a mistake worth naming
before anyone starts generating. Pentiment is set around 1500 and its style is
its subject: the game is about manuscript illumination and the coming of print,
so an illuminated hand IS the world it depicts. Borrow that hand for 1775 and
the picture is 250 years out of date — a Bavarian scriptorium drawing the siege
of Boston. It would read as a costume, and this project's whole discipline is
that the picture is true.

So the sources are the print culture this war actually had, which is a drawn,
contoured, flat-coloured culture in its own right:

  - Amos Doolittle's four engravings of Lexington and Concord, 1775 — already a
    findable object in CB-03 and already the game's argument about the visual
    record. Flat, frontal, naive in exactly the useful way.
  - Broadside and newspaper woodcuts: heavy contour, no tone, no perspective.
  - Pen-and-wash topographical views by military engineers — the original
    direction in docs/02, and the way an officer actually recorded ground.
  - Hand-coloured English aquatint views of towns and harbours.

Every one of them is line first and colour second. That is the whole brief.

=============================================================== THE STYLE BLOCK

Paste verbatim. This replaces style-anchor-oil-v1 for environments if this
medium is adopted; it does not touch the dialogue portraits.

STYLE: a hand-drawn illustration in the manner of an eighteenth-century engraved
view that has been coloured by hand. Every form is bounded by a drawn ink
contour in warm brown-black, and the colour sits inside that contour as a flat,
opaque, slightly uneven wash with no blending, no gradient, no highlight and no
cast shadow. The line is a quill line: it varies in weight along its length, it
wavers, and at the corners it overshoots slightly rather than closing exactly.
Nothing is rendered and nothing is modelled — a wall is a shape with a line
round it and two or three drawn courses of stone to say what it is made of, not
a surface with light falling across it.

THE SPACE: flattened and frontal, in the manner of a period engraved view.
Depth is stated by overlap and by height on the page, not by atmospheric haze
and not by dramatic converging perspective. Buildings are drawn nearly square to
the viewer. Distant things are smaller and higher up, and they are drawn with
the same weight of line as near things rather than fading out.

THE DETAIL: drawn incident everywhere, and all of it made of line — courses of
stone, ranks of roof shingles, the boards of a fence, tufts of grass, the ruts
of a track, the weave of a tent's canvas. This is what separates the style from
flat vector art: the picture is busy with drawn marks and empty of rendering.

THE COLOUR: a warm parchment ground, and on it a small, muted, earthy palette —
olive green, ochre, dull brick red, weathered timber brown, cold slate, and
unbleached linen. Colours are laid flat and are allowed to pool slightly uneven
within a shape. Nothing is pure black and nothing is pure white; the darkest
value in the picture is the ink of the contour.

=========================================================== NEGATIVE, ADAPTED

Use negative-oil-v1 in full — every entry on it still applies — and add:

ADDITIONAL NEGATIVE: illuminated manuscript, gold leaf, gilding, marginalia,
drop capital, decorated initial, vellum, medieval, gothic, Book of Hours,
scriptorium, monk, friar, Bavarian, half-timbered house, tonsure,
oil painting, brush texture, impasto, painterly, soft edges, blended shading,
cel shading with a hard terminator, ambient occlusion, drop shadow, gradient
sky, sunset, volumetric light, watercolour bleed, wet edges,
flat vector, clip art, infographic, sticker, thick uniform outline of even
weight, perfectly closed contour, geometric precision, ruler-straight edges

  The last line is the one that will need policing. The failure mode of this
  style is not ugliness, it is TIDINESS: a machine draws a contour of perfectly
  even weight that closes exactly, and the result reads as clip art. If a sample
  comes back looking like a vector illustration, the fix is always the same —
  ask for the line to wander and vary and overshoot.

======================================================= COMPOSITION, UNCHANGED

Everything in READY-scene-plates-v1.txt still governs, because those rules are
about the CUT and not the medium: the horizon exactly one third down as a clean
unbroken horizontal, the walkable ground open and unobstructed, no people
anywhere in the frame, a near-field mass cropped by the bottom edge, generous
margin on all four sides, and no legible lettering.

One addition specific to this medium. The near-field mass must be stated in
LINE and dark colour rather than in value alone — a bank of drawn earth with
ruts and stones in it, a fence of drawn boards, a cart. In a flat style there is
no atmospheric perspective to separate the foreground from the ground behind it,
so if the foreground is only a darker shape the frame will read as two stripes.

============================================================ WHY THIS PAIRS

The figure sheets are already line-and-fill: closed ink contour, flat colour,
no rendering. That was chosen for legibility at eighty pixels and it left the
oil plates as the odd thing in the frame — a drawn man standing in a painting.

In this medium the world is drawn in the man's own language, so he belongs to
it. That is the entire argument, and /bakeoff.html demonstrates it against four
alternatives with the real cut sprite standing in each.

What it costs, stated plainly: the alla prima direction in docs/09 goes, and
with it the atmospheric range that a painted ground gives — this style cannot do
weather, or distance, or a sodden grey morning, by tone. It has to do them by
palette and by drawn incident instead. The mood shader must be re-tuned for it:
draining the colour out of a flat drawn picture reads as fading, not as gloom.
