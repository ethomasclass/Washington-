SHEET 1 — Mount Vernon, May 1775 · Martha Washington + Lund Washington
PAINTERLY VARIANT — the oil-sketch register, not the pen-and-wash one.

NOTE ON THE ANCHOR. This describes visual qualities and names only
public-domain painters, in the same way 02 §6 anchors Washington's face to
Peale because Peale is public domain. It does not instruct the model to
imitate a living artist or a named studio's house style. The look you want
comes out of the qualities — loaded brush, dissolving edges, acid chroma
against a muted ground, hard chiaroscuro — not out of a name.

Everything historical here is unchanged from the pen-and-wash version: the
costume detail, the period locks and the whole negative list are style-
independent and stay exactly as they are.

--------------------------------------------------------------------- PROMPT

An oil sketch of two portrait studies side by side on one canvas, at
identical scale and under identical light, each a chest-up study with clear
space around it and no overlap between them.

LEFT: Martha Washington, aged forty-three, a small, round-faced,
plainly-handsome Virginia gentlewoman with brown hair and hazel eyes, a
short-necked and comfortably built woman who has been rich her whole adult
life and dresses like it only in the quality of the cloth. She wears a plain
gown of good brown silk with elbow-length sleeves and fine white ruffles at
the elbow, a white linen neckerchief crossed at the breast and pinned high at
the throat, and a white linen cap with a ruffled edge covering her hair
completely and tied under the chin. Her hair is dressed back beneath the cap
and none of it is loose. Her expression is direct, intelligent and entirely
unsentimental.

RIGHT: Lund Washington, aged thirty-eight, estate manager and cousin, a lean,
weathered, sandy-haired Virginia planter's son in working dress: a plain
russet-brown broadcloth coat with no lace and horn buttons, a drab waistcoat,
a plain linen stock, and his own unpowdered hair tied at the nape with a
black ribbon. Sunburn to the line of his collar and pale above it. A careful,
patient, slightly evasive face — a man used to writing letters explaining why
something has not been finished.

STYLE: alla prima oil painting on a mid-toned ground, in the manner of
nineteenth-century Russian and Spanish realist portrait sketches — Ilya
Repin, Valentin Serov, Joaquin Sorolla, John Singer Sargent's oil studies.
Painted wet-into-wet with a loaded brush and left unfinished. The paint is
thick and visibly worked where the light falls — on the brow, the cheekbone,
the knuckle of the nose, the linen at the throat — and thins to bare stained
ground in the shadows and at the edges of the figure, where the drawing
dissolves into open brushwork and is not closed. Big confident strokes that
describe form in one pass and are not blended out. The likeness is carried by
paint quality and by structure, never by fine detail: no rendered eyelashes,
no rendered hair strands, no polished surfaces.

COLOUR: a muted, dirty, earth-dominated ground — raw umber, olive, cool grey,
ochre — carrying a small number of unnaturally saturated accents that do not
belong to the natural light: a sour green in the shadow of the linen, a
bruised violet under the jaw, a hot orange note at the edge of the flesh.
Those accents are placed, deliberate and few. The overall image reads as
sombre and slightly unwell, never pretty and never warm-nostalgic.

LIGHT: one hard low-angle source, strongly directional, carving the face into
a clear lit mass and a large soft shadow mass. Deep shadow that stays
transparent and coloured, never black and never dead. Some edges lost
completely into the background where the shadow side meets the ground.

GROUND: a flat unrendered field of scumbled dirty grey-green, worked with the
same brush as the figures, with no scenery, no drapery, no column, no sky, no
horizon and no frame. The canvas texture shows through in places. Nothing in
the image is pure black and nothing is pure white.
