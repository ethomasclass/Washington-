03a §0 already says there is exactly one canonical generation per plate, and
that L0–L5 are compositional zones inside that one image rather than six
separate generations. src/scenery.ts is the other half of that instruction: it
cuts the single painting into the bands the renderer parallaxes.

============================================== WHAT THE CUT NEEDS FROM THE IMAGE

Five composition rules. Everything else is 03a as written. Break any of these
and the cut still runs — it just produces a scene the player cannot walk in.

1. THE HORIZON SITS ONE THIRD DOWN, and it must be a clean, unbroken horizontal.
   ground.ts locks HORIZON at 0.34 project-wide and every other number in the
   game — figure scale, the walk plane, where a tent's ridge lands — is measured
   from it. The cutter scales and shifts the painting to put the painted horizon
   on the engine's, and `ScenerySpec.horizon` is where you tell it what fraction
   of the source the horizon actually landed at. Get that number wrong and the
   figures look like they are floating.

2. THE GROUND BELOW THE HORIZON IS OPEN AND UNOBSTRUCTED. That band is where the
   player walks. Nothing may stand in it that a person would have to walk round,
   because nothing painted into a band can be walked behind. Objects the player
   names and examines are props composited at runtime (03a §4.0.2), not paint.

3. NO PEOPLE ANYWHERE IN THE WALKABLE BAND. Every figure in this game is a
   billboarded cutout placed at runtime. A painted man in the middle distance is
   a man who never moves, is always the same size, and cannot be spoken to. A
   body of troops far enough away that individual men are not separable is one
   FORM, not a hundred figures, and is allowed at the horizon only.

4. A NEAR-FIELD OCCLUDER ACROSS THE BOTTOM EDGE — an earth bank, a cart, a
   hedge, a table edge — cropped by the frame, at the heaviest ink weight and
   with almost no colour in it. The cutter lifts the bottom 14% as the front
   layer, so it passes in front of the player and gives the frame its depth. If
   the painting has nothing there, that band lifts a strip of empty ground and
   the scene reads flat.

5. GENEROUS MARGIN ON ALL FOUR SIDES, nothing important within 8% of any edge.
   The bands slide against each other as the player walks, and the edges are
   what slide out of frame.

Also, as ever: no lettering anywhere. Any writing shown is illegible strokes.

Deliver 16:9. 2048 x 1152 or larger. The cut fits width and lets the top and
bottom margins take up the slack.

========================================================= THE CAMP — worked example

Every other scene is this with a different SUBJECT and LIGHT. Take the subject
lines from 05 §2.3 and change nothing else.

SUBJECT:
The American camp on the common outside Cambridge, Massachusetts, in July 1775.
A shanty-town pretending to be an army: shelters of boards, of sailcloth, of
board and sailcloth mixed, of stone and turf, of birch, of brush, pitched where
their makers liked and facing every direction. Stage right, one regiment's
proper canvas wedge tents stand in ordered rows — the single thing in the
picture that looks like a military camp, and it makes everything around it look
worse. A cooking fire, a washing line, a wood pile. Beyond the camp the ground
falls to the water, and across the water lies Boston: a low grey town of roofs
and steeples, with ships at anchor in the stream, small and precise and out of
reach.

COMPOSITION:
A shallow elevated three-quarter view, the camera at the height of a standing
man on slightly higher ground, looking level. The horizon runs unbroken across
the frame exactly one third down from the top. Below it the open ground of the
camp street runs from the bottom edge to the horizon, worn by feet, empty of
people, and clear of any obstruction the whole way. The shelters stand to left
and right of that open ground, none of them crossing it. In the immediate
foreground, cropped by the bottom edge, a rutted bank of churned earth with a
broken barrel and a length of fence, drawn at the heaviest ink weight and with
almost no colour in it. The picture is flat and frontal, with no wide-angle
distortion and no converging verticals.

LIGHT:
Flat overcast with no directional key and no visible sun. Shadows are soft,
short and cool. Nothing in the frame is brightly lit and nothing is in deep
shade. Mid-morning in high summer, the air heavy and still.

PALETTE:
The paper ground is a dirty grey-green. Earth-dominated and muted throughout —
raw umber, olive, cold ochre, wet stone grey, unbleached linen. The only
saturated colour permitted in this frame is the dull warm red of a regimental
flag at the tent lines, occupying almost none of the picture. No blue in the
uniforms: there are no uniforms. Nothing is pure black and nothing is pure
white.

TECHNICAL:
Composed with generous empty margin on all four sides, with nothing important
near any edge. Any writing shown is illegible: fine ink strokes suggesting
cursive, not readable letterforms. No people anywhere in the picture. The open
ground the figures will stand on is clear and unobstructed.

=============================================================== DROPPING IT IN

1. Save as  public/plates/camp.jpg   (or .png). The names are the plate-set
   names the scenes already carry: vernon, camp, lines, parlour.
2. Reload. The scene builds on the procedural plates as always, and swaps the
   painting in when it has been cut — so a missing or unreadable file simply
   leaves the placeholder showing.
3. Measure where the horizon actually landed, as a fraction of image height, and
   set it in `SCENERY` in src/scenery.ts. If figures look like they are floating
   above the ground or sunk into it, that number is wrong and nothing else is.

================================================================== ACCEPTANCE

The six from 09 §7, and four more that only matter because of the cut:

7.  Is the horizon a clean unbroken horizontal, and is it one third down?
8.  Walk the player from the front of the frame to the back. Does he pass
    anything painted that he should have gone behind? If so, that object wants
    to be a prop, not paint.
9.  Walk him left and right. Does any seam between bands become visible as a
    line, or open a gap? If it does, raise the `bleed` on the band above it.
10. Squint. Is there a foreground mass across the bottom edge, dark and almost
    colourless? Without it the frame has no depth for the parallax to show.
