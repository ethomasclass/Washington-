Assembled in the fixed order of 03b §0.2:
  [SUBJECT] [char-style-block-oil-v1] [FRAMING-FIGURE] [negative-oil-v1]

The negative list goes in EVERY call (09 §6). No exceptions, no abbreviating.

=============================================================== WHAT THE ENGINE WANTS

These numbers are not preferences. They are read straight out of the code, and
art that misses them cannot be dropped in without being re-cut.

FACINGS — THREE, NOT FOUR OR EIGHT.
  front, side, back.
  The engine mirrors the side cycle on the x axis to walk the other way
  (renderer.ts setFacing/setPlayerPos: `this.player.scale.x = -scale.x`), so a
  right-facing set is not merely unnecessary, it is WRONG to supply — two
  different drawings of the same man walking opposite ways will read as a
  costume change every time he turns round.
  Draw the side cycle FACING FRAME-LEFT. That is the direction the engine
  assumes and mirrors from.

FRAMES — NINE PER FACING.
  Cell 1 is the idle stand. Cells 2-9 are one complete walk cycle, eight evenly
  spaced phases, cell 9 leading back into cell 2 without a jump.
  (art.ts washingtonFrames: `[washington(facing, -1), ...8 phases]`.)
  The gait is advanced by DISTANCE WALKED, not by time, so the cycle has to be
  even: an eight-frame cycle with two lazy frames in it will visibly limp.

PROPORTION — 0.46 WIDE TO 1 TALL.
  ground.ts FIGURE_ASPECT = 0.46. The cell is that ratio, and the figure is
  drawn to fill it. A figure drawn squarer than this gets squeezed.

SILHOUETTE — READ AT EIGHTY PIXELS.
  In play a man is about eighty pixels tall and moving. The hat, the coat flare
  and the stance carry the whole character; a face costs nothing and returns
  nothing. STATE.md's standing note applies: everything opaque, no translucency
  anywhere in a figure.

BACKGROUND — MUST COME OUT.
  Figures are billboarded cutouts composited over the painted plates
  (`transparent: true` textures), so the background has to be removable
  cleanly. Generate on a FLAT, EVEN, UNIFORM field of dirty grey-green
  (#6E6A57 or near) with no scenery, no vignette, no cast shadow on the ground,
  and no gradient — then key it out in post.

  THE ONE REAL TENSION WITH 09. The medium spec wants at least one major edge
  lost into the ground. A cutout cannot lose its outer edge — key it out and a
  lost edge becomes a hole in the man. So the rule is amended for figures only,
  and only here: LOSE EDGES INSIDE THE FIGURE — the shadow side of the coat
  into the breeches, the hat brim into the hair — and keep the OUTER SILHOUETTE
  closed and readable the whole way round. Nothing else in 09 is relaxed.

SHEET — 3 x 3, ONE FACING PER SHEET.
  Following the production guide's prop-sheet precedent: one 1536 x 1536
  generation, nine cells, read left to right and top to bottom. Nine cells is
  exactly idle-plus-eight, so a facing is one generation and the three facings
  share light, palette, ink weight and paper because each sheet is internally
  consistent.

  Cell order:      1 2 3        1 = idle
                   4 5 6        2..9 = walk phases in order
                   7 8 9

  In every cell: the figure is centred left to right, the FEET SIT ON A COMMON
  BASELINE at the same height in every cell, the head has the same clearance
  above it in every cell, and the figure is the same height in every cell. The
  man must not grow, shrink or drift up and down the cell between frames — that
  is the single most common way a generated sheet becomes unusable.
  No grid lines, no numbers, no labels, no captions drawn on the sheet.

=============================================================== ORDER OF WORK

Generating nine consistent frames of the same man in one shot is the hard part,
and no model does it reliably from text alone. Do it in this order:

  1. THE LOCK. One image, one figure, full length, standing: Washington front
     on. This is the character reference and nothing else is generated until it
     is right. Judge it against the acceptance list at the foot of this file.
  2. THE OTHER TWO STANDS. Side and back, generated WITH THE LOCK AS AN IMAGE
     REFERENCE, not from the text alone.
  3. THE CYCLES. One 3x3 sheet per facing, each generated with its own stand as
     the image reference, and with the instruction that the costume, build,
     palette and light are identical in all nine cells and only the legs, arms
     and coat-skirt move.
  4. THE NPC SHEET. Nine different people, one sheet, standing only.

If a model will not hold the character across nine cells, fall back to three
cells per generation (contact, passing, contact) and interpolate the rest in
post — the cycle is symmetrical, so five drawn phases give eight usable frames.

=============================================================== SHEET A — WASHINGTON, THE LOCK

SUBJECT:
A single full-length figure of George Washington in June 1775, aged
forty-three, standing at ease and facing the viewer square on. He is
extraordinarily tall for the period and built long — six foot two, wide in the
shoulder, narrow in the hip, with big hands and large feet, and he stands
absolutely straight without stiffness, a man who has been the tallest person in
every room of his life and is used to it. He wears the buff-and-blue uniform he
chose himself: a dark blue broadcloth coat cut away at the front with buff
facings, buff lapels turned back, deep buff cuffs, and plain gilt buttons; a
buff waistcoat; buff breeches; white stockings; black leather shoes with plain
buckles. A plain white linen stock at the throat and a small white ruffle at the
breast. His own hair, unpowdered, dressed back from the forehead and clubbed at
the nape with a black ribbon. A plain small-sword hangs at his left hip. His
expression is closed, watchful and entirely without warmth.
He wears a plain black cocked hat, worn square, with no plume, no lace and no
cockade.

  HAT NOTE — decide before generating, then keep it. STATE.md's rough-edge list
  says he is bareheaded in the current reference and that a hat is a much
  stronger silhouette at eighty pixels, which is the size that matters. This
  prompt therefore hats him. If the hat is dropped, drop it in ALL nine cells of
  ALL three facings and in the Act 2 riband variant too.

  ACT 2 VARIANT — the same figure with a broad LIGHT BLUE silk riband worn
  across the breast from the right shoulder to the left hip, over the coat. He
  adopted it at Cambridge to be identifiable as commander-in-chief. Generate it
  as a separate pass from the same lock so the two states are the same man.

[char-style-block-oil-v1 — verbatim]

FRAMING-FIGURE:
The whole figure, head to feet, with the feet on the lower edge of the picture
and clear space above the head. Straight-on eye level, no upward camera, no
low heroic angle, no perspective distortion. The figure is centred and fills
the height of the frame. Nothing else in the picture: no ground plane, no
horizon, no cast shadow, no props, no scenery. A flat even field of dirty
grey-green behind, worked with the same brush, edge to edge, with no vignette
and no gradient. The outer silhouette of the figure stays closed and readable
all the way round; edges are lost only inside the figure.

=============================================================== SHEET B — A WALK CYCLE

Run three times, once per facing, each with its own stand as image reference.

SUBJECT:
A sheet of nine studies of the same single figure in a three by three grid, at
identical scale, under identical light, in identical dress, with clear space
around each and no overlap. The man is [ THE LOCK DESCRIPTION, IN FULL ].

Cell 1: standing at ease, weight even, arms at his sides.
Cells 2 to 9: one complete walking cycle in eight even phases —
  2 left heel strikes, weight forward, right arm forward
  3 weight rolls onto the left foot, right leg swinging through
  4 passing position, legs together, body at its highest
  5 right leg reaching forward, left heel lifting
  6 right heel strikes, weight forward, left arm forward
  7 weight rolls onto the right foot, left leg swinging through
  8 passing position again, the opposite leg leading
  9 left leg reaching forward, carrying back into cell 2 without a jump
The coat skirt swings behind the leading leg and settles at the passing
positions. The body rises at the passing positions and drops at each heel
strike. Nothing else changes between cells: same costume, same colours, same
light, same build, same height, same hat.

FACING — use exactly one of these lines:
  FRONT: the figure faces the viewer square on and walks toward the viewer.
  SIDE:  the figure is in true profile FACING FRAME-LEFT and walks to frame
         left. The far arm and far leg are visible and are painted a value
         darker than the near ones.
  BACK:  the figure is seen from directly behind and walks away from the
         viewer. The face is not visible at all.

=============================================================== SHEET C — THE NPCs

One 1536 x 1536 sheet, three by three, nine different people, standing only —
the engine varies pose procedurally and only needs their stands.

SUBJECT:
A sheet of nine full-length figure studies in a three by three grid, nine
different men of the American camp before Boston in 1775, at identical scale,
under identical light, each standing plainly with clear space around it and no
overlap. No two are alike in build, height, age, colouring or dress. None of
them is in a uniform: this is an army that has not been issued anything.

  1 a New England farmer of fifty in a homespun drab coat and a round felt hat,
    heavy through the shoulders, worn boots
  2 a boy of seventeen, thin, in a coat plainly cut down from his father's, no
    hat, hair loose
  3 a Virginia rifleman in a fringed linen hunting shirt to mid-thigh, a
    leather belt, leggings, a round hat, lean and long-limbed
  4 a Rhode Island private in the nearest thing to uniform on the sheet, a
    brown coat with facings that nearly match his neighbour's, a cocked hat
  5 a sergeant of forty, thickset, in a good russet coat, a cocked hat worn
    square, a halberd grounded at his side
  6 a labourer stripped to shirt and waistcoat with the sleeves rolled, no
    coat, no hat, forearms burned to the elbow
  7 an officer of the Massachusetts line in a blue coat with red facings, a
    cocked hat, a small-sword, thirty and pleased with himself
  8 a wagoner in a long drab overcoat and a broad round hat, older, stooped
  9 a free Black soldier of the Massachusetts line — men served in these
    regiments from Lexington onward — a plain brown coat, a round hat, a
    musket grounded, thirty, upright and unremarkable in exactly the way the
    other eight are unremarkable

[char-style-block-oil-v1] [FRAMING-FIGURE, nine cells, common baseline]
[negative-oil-v1 + the figure additions]

  Cell 9 is not decoration and is not a modern insertion. Free Black men served
  in the New England regiments from the first day. Drawn plainly, at the same
  scale and with the same lack of ceremony as the other eight, it teaches by
  being unremarkable — which is the register the whole project works in.

=============================================================== DROPPING IT IN

1. Key out the grey-green field. Keep the outer silhouette closed; check at
   100% that no limb has been eaten.
2. Cut on the cell grid. Trim every cell to the SAME box — do not crop each
   figure to its own bounds, or the man will jitter as the cycle plays.
3. Save as public/figures/<asset id>.webp
4. The engine currently draws these procedurally in art.ts (washington(),
   characterCutout()). Consuming a sheet needs a loader that slices the grid and
   hands the renderer nine textures per facing, exactly as washingtonFrames()
   returns today — same shape, different source. That is a small piece of
   engine work and is not yet written.

============================================== ACCEPTANCE (09 §7, plus figures)

1. Is there a lost edge INSIDE the figure — and is the outer silhouette closed?
2. Does the mid-toned ground show through in the thin passages?
3. Three or four saturated accents. Count them. Not zero, not everywhere.
4. Are the shadows coloured and transparent rather than neutral black?
5. Could this be a nineteenth-century history painting? If yes, reject.
6. Is it true? Buff and blue, no epaulettes on both shoulders in 1775, no
   powdered wig, own hair clubbed, no beard, feet not cropped.
7. Squint until it is eighty pixels tall. Is it still a person, and still THAT
   person? If it is a smudge, the silhouette has failed and the drawing does
   not matter.
8. Flip cell 9 to cell 2. Does the cycle close without a jump?
